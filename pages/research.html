When the Cloud Comes to Town: The Local Air Pollution of Data Center Construction

Abstract: This paper estimates the causal effect of data center construction on local air pollution. Data centers must test backup diesel generators after receiving air permits during construction, and that commissioning emits SO2. I combine facility-level permit data with NASA satellite-based grid-level SO2 measurements from 2005--2024 and estimate the effect of data center entry using a stacked staggered difference-in-differences design. In the first year after permit issuance, SO2 rises by 0.066 ppb, or 0.078 standard deviation. Using QCEW data, I also find evidence of a \$4.5 increase in average weekly wages and a 0.3-percentage-point increase in employment share following the first data center entering a county. A back-of-the-envelope welfare calculation implies a modest welfare gain in the first year, considering only mortality risk from SO2 increase and labor market benefits
